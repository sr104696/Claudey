# New job-radar discovery ideas

**Purpose:** Expand the radar's recall in directions that fit Seth's actual strengths but are easy
to miss when searching only for "legal analyst," "regulatory counsel," or a handful of known
employers. This is a discovery brief, not a claim that any source below currently has a live feed
or permits automated polling. Re-check each source's current terms, robots.txt, endpoint and
availability before implementing it.

The existing project rule remains controlling: broad discovery produces **leads**, never proof.
Only a current fetch of an open employer/official posting can put a position in the verified list.
No login, credential reuse, CAPTCHA or paywall bypass, or scraping of LinkedIn, Indeed or Glassdoor.

## The main expansion idea: search by work product, not just job title

The candidate's strongest common denominator is not one industry label. It is the ability to read
messy primary material, identify what matters, and write a decision-useful assessment. That work
appears under titles such as **diligence, intelligence, research, investigations, content,
surveillance, policy, claims, governance, and evaluation**—sometimes outside legal departments.
Discover those postings broadly, then use the posting text and current rubric to reject sales,
operations, quant and unrelated practice work.

## Employers and sector maps not obvious from the current list

Build target universes from sector maps, then verify each named employer's real career URL and ATS.
Do not seed guessed ATS slugs as verified.

1. **Specialty legal-risk capital:** litigation funders and insurers, legal-expenses/ATE providers,
   litigation-risk underwriters, judgment-enforcement finance, mass-tort finance and legal-claims
   investors. Include both capital providers and specialist brokers/advisers; the latter may have
   research roles even when the fund itself is small.
2. **Insurance and reinsurance:** specialty lines, professional liability, D&O, cyber, litigation
   coverage, political risk and trade credit. Search insurers, reinsurers, brokers and risk-data
   vendors separately; their titles and ATS estates differ.
3. **Proxy, governance and stewardship research:** proxy-advisory firms, stewardship/data vendors,
   shareholder intelligence providers and corporate-governance research teams inside asset
   managers. Screen carefully for financial modeling requirements.
4. **Risk and diligence information providers:** political-risk and geopolitical intelligence,
   corporate investigations, beneficial-ownership data, expert networks, due-diligence research,
   fraud intelligence and litigation/court-data providers. Look for analyst/editor/research seats,
   not field investigators or sales.
5. **Financial market infrastructure:** exchanges, clearinghouses, custodians, payment networks,
   broker-dealers, market makers and trade repositories. Search legal, regulatory affairs,
   market-conduct, member-policy and rulemaking research functions—not just "counsel."
6. **Regulatory-policy ecosystem:** state financial regulators, federal agencies, SROs, standards
   bodies, financial trade associations, policy institutes and university centers. Include
   research contractors and public-interest organizations where pay and location are workable.
7. **Credit and corporate-event data:** ratings, loan-market, covenant, restructuring, corporate
   actions, bankruptcy-court data and issuer-disclosure vendors. Add data publishers and workflow
   software firms to the employer map, not only the best-known credit-intelligence brands.
8. **AI assurance for regulated industries:** model-risk and responsible-AI teams at banks,
   insurers, exchanges, legal-data vendors and AI auditors. Separate internal policy/research from
   model development and customer implementation.
9. **Expert/advisory and litigation-support firms:** economic/forensic consulting, damages and
   investigations firms, e-discovery/litigation-data vendors and expert-network research groups.
   Include only bounded research or advisory seats; many neighboring roles are billable litigation,
   sales, or spreadsheet-heavy analysis.
10. **Public-company governance and activist intelligence:** proxy contests, activist defense,
    shareholder intelligence and corporate governance research. This is a source for unusual
    research jobs, not a reason to imply existing activist-investing experience.

For each family, discover employer names from professional-association member directories that
are public, conference exhibitor/sponsor lists, regulator/SRO registries, public vendor directories,
ratings/data-provider lists, public fund portfolios and citations in relevant research. Use those
pages as **employer-discovery signals**; fetch their career pages separately. Do not crawl member-only
directories or infer that a sponsor actually has a relevant vacancy.

## More ways to find fresh postings

### 1. Sweep the career pages already known, not only the ATS families already implemented

- For every registry row, keep a checked career-page URL even when its ATS is unsupported or
  blocked. Revisit that public landing page on a low cadence and record "page reachable, no
  supported feed" distinctly from "no jobs" and "not searched."
- When permitted, inspect same-host `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, sitemap indexes, RSS/Atom links,
  JobPosting JSON-LD and links to documented public ATS boards. Restrict any sitemap expansion to
  the employer's own host and job-like URLs; cap it, cache it, and verify final jobs individually.
- Add ATS detection coverage only after confirming the employer-facing public endpoint and
  allowed access. Candidate families to **investigate**, not presume scrapeable: iCIMS, Jobvite,
  Avature, Taleo, Dayforce, UKG, ADP, Phenom, Eightfold, Teamtailor, Personio and custom
  career-site JSON-LD. If automation is not permitted or cannot be verified, preserve the employer
  as a manual-check target instead of guessing.
- A public employer page may reveal a documented JSON feed even when the ATS brand is unknown.
  Store the landing page, feed URL, terms/robots check, and date checked so future runs can
  distinguish an intentional skip from a connector failure.

### 2. Add source types before adding lots of one-off scrapers

- **Public job feeds:** RSS, Atom and JSON Feed; official API endpoints; employer-documented ATS
  job-board feeds; public government vacancy feeds. Register feed URL, publisher, format,
  permitted cadence, last success and failure reason in one catalog.
- **Public listing pages:** narrow, low-frequency parsers for niche boards that permit automated
  access. Emit title/company/URL/date only; do not use the aggregator page as final evidence.
- **Search-provider discovery:** use the existing human-operated WebSearch replay for broad and
  niche query packs. Search engines are useful for discovery, but snippets, cached text and job
  cards are never verification. Keep an auditable query ID and import the result URL, not just a
  copied snippet.
- **Board change detection:** compare current public ATS board IDs against the prior run and
  prioritize newly posted IDs, changed descriptions and recently re-opened roles. This can surface
  fresh jobs without repeatedly re-reading every unchanged description.
- **Employer announcements:** public press releases, official newsletters and company "we're
  hiring" pages can identify a team or a new board. Treat them as a pointer to a vacancy, not a
  vacancy unless they link to an open posting.
- **User-controlled referrals and alerts:** support a local import for exported public links or
  user-forwarded job alerts. Keep source provenance and private files git-ignored; never connect
  directly to a login-gated university, mailbox or community account.

### 3. Search many vocabularies and board families

Rotate query variants across titles, work products, domains, and employer types. Examples for the
existing WebSearch workflow:

```text
"legal assets" OR "litigation risk" (underwriting OR diligence OR research) jobs
"proxy research" OR "corporate governance research" analyst New York
"political risk" OR "regulatory intelligence" analyst financial services New York
"market conduct" OR "market structure policy" research analyst exchange
"AI governance" OR "model risk policy" (lawyer OR JD OR research) bank
"regulatory examination" (strategy OR policy OR research) credit risk jobs
"special situations" OR "event-driven" legal research analyst
"professional liability" OR "litigation coverage" claims research counsel
"court data" OR "litigation intelligence" legal research analyst
"legal research editor" OR "legal content analyst" AI jobs
site:jobs.lever.co OR site:jobs.ashbyhq.com "counterparty risk" legal OR policy
site:myworkdayjobs.com "regulatory affairs" analyst exchange OR payments OR bank
```

These are starting strings, not a new mandatory CSV dump. Test query yield against canonical
verified postings, deduplicate semantic variants, and retire strings that repeatedly return only
stale or irrelevant material. Add phrases from actual relevant postings to a versioned vocabulary.

## Prioritization and measurement

Prioritize the next expansion by **incremental verified relevant postings per engineering hour**,
not raw lead count. For each source/query/family record:

- last attempted, last successful, HTTP/robots/terms outcome and cadence;
- raw leads, unique canonical employer URLs, verified-open count, rubric-fit count and poor-match
  count;
- canonical resolution rate, duplicate rate, median posting age at discovery and fraction that are
  already closed;
- the role family, employer segment and location that produced the result.

Keep at least three states distinct: **searched, no result**; **not covered**; and **blocked or
failed**. A high volume of leads with a low employer-verification rate is not successful coverage.
Revisit source priority quarterly using useful/not-useful outcomes, but do not silently relax hard
exclusions to inflate yield.

Suggested rollout:

1. Expand title/work-product query packs and add adjacent employer families to the registry with
   explicit source URLs and segments.
2. Add a generic, constrained sitemap/JSON-LD and feed catalog where access is permitted; make
   each source's health visible in the run log.
3. Add direct ATS adapters only for repeatedly valuable unsupported employer families, each with
   fixtures, pagination, failure isolation and current-posting verification.
4. Add local user-export imports for private alerts; keep portal-only listings clearly unverified
   and separate from the public verified list.
5. Use yield metrics to promote sources that find new, verified roles and retire noisy dead ends.

## Guardrail checklist for any new source

- Is the specific page/API public, documented or clearly offered for automation, and permitted by
  its terms? Check current robots.txt; do not assume public access means permission to crawl.
- Does the shared polite client enforce per-host spacing, redirects, caching, transparent User-Agent
  and block logging? Honor a longer publisher crawl delay and API-specific quota.
- Is the source a lead source or authoritative employer source? Preserve both URLs and provenance.
- Can one malformed response or a single bad posting fail only that item rather than the whole run?
- Does the final row have a current employer/official fetch proving the listing remains open?
- Does the new vocabulary find the candidate's kind of judgment work without implying finance,
  modeling, legal-practice or trading qualifications he does not have?

**Do not implement blindly:** public APIs may have terms, quotas, stale data or robots restrictions;
web-server CORS policy is not permission and is not the relevant control for this server-side client.
Check the publisher's terms, robots rules, documentation and rate limits before each connector.
